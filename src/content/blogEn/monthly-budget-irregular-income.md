Variable income needs a different starting point: build the month around a conservative income floor, not the best invoice you received.

## Set the floor and fund fixed costs

Review recent payments and choose a cautious baseline. It covers housing, insurance, food, debt payments, and a monthly allowance for annual costs. Keep a separate smoothing reserve for the gap between that floor and a low month.

## Example: four uneven months

A freelancer receives €1,800, €2,600, €1,500, and €3,100. Their working budget is €1,700: €1,250 fixed costs, €300 living costs, and €150 irregular costs. The €900 and €1,400 available in the high months replenish the reserve; the €1,500 month uses €200 of it rather than delaying a bill.

## Give excess income a rule

After the current month is covered, direct excess income to the smoothing reserve, irregular-cost pots, then savings goals. A rule such as “70% of income above €1,700 goes to reserves” stops a strong month from creating a permanent new commitment. Test the floor with the [monthly budget calculator](/en/finance/monthly-budget/).

For the size of a safety target, see [how much to keep in an emergency fund](/en/blog/emergency-fund-guide/).
