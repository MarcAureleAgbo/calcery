An inflation adjustment starts with bills and receipts, not a blanket cut to every category.

## Compare the old budget with recent spending

Keep a reference column beside the last three months. In one household, groceries rose from €420 to €475, energy from €115 to €145, and fuel from €90 to €125: a €120 monthly change. Rent and insurance did not rise, so they are not part of this diagnosis.

## Protect essentials, then make trade-offs

Treat housing, minimum energy use, required transport, food, and insurance as constrained costs. Review flexible categories for a cheaper plan, a negotiation, a substitution, or a genuine cancellation. In this example, ending an €18 subscription, cutting €22 from a phone plan, and reducing restaurant spending by €80 covers the change without pretending every cost is optional.

## Check the new plan

After two billing cycles, compare planned and actual spending. If groceries still exceed the cap, raise it explicitly and offset another line. The [monthly budget calculator](/en/finance/monthly-budget/) can test the new balance; it cannot predict local prices.
